## Summary
Festival celebrating the bond between brothers and sisters. Sisters tie a sacred thread (rakhi) on their brothers' wrists as a symbol of protection and blessings.

## Significance
Raksha Bandhan celebrates the protective bond between siblings. The rakhi, a sacred thread, symbolizes the sister's love and the brother's commitment to protect her. This ancient festival is celebrated across India and among diaspora communities worldwide, reinforcing family bonds and mutual responsibilities.

## Vidhi (Steps)
**Step 1: Set up the Altar**
Prepare a clean space and arrange flowers, diya, water, and the rakhi. Create a simple altar with the rakhi as the central element. This is the sacred space for the ritual.

**Step 2: Light the Lamp and Offer Prayers**
Light the diya and offer flowers and water. Recite prayers for the brother's safety, health, and prosperity. The sister should offer these prayers with heartfelt devotion, invoking blessings for the brother.

**Step 3: Apply Tilak to Brother's Forehead**
Mix vermillion with rice and apply a tilak on the brother's forehead. This marks him as blessed and protected by his sister's prayers.

**Step 4: Tie the Rakhi**
With prayers and blessings, tie the rakhi (sacred thread) on the brother's wrist. This is the central ritual of Raksha Bandhan. As the sister ties the rakhi, she recites blessings for the brother's protection and prosperity. The brother accepts this with reverence.

**Step 5: Exchange of Gifts and Blessings**
After tying the rakhi, the brother offers gifts and sweets to the sister. He blesses her and reaffirms his commitment to protect her. The sister accepts these with joy. Distribute sweets and gifts among family members to celebrate the occasion.

## Regional Variations
**Coastal India Variation (Nariyel Purnima)** (Regions: west)
In coastal regions, particularly in Maharashtra and Goa, this festival is also known as Nariyel Purnima (coconut full moon). The ritual includes offering coconuts to the sea, believed to appease the sea God. Fishing communities perform a special ritual offering coconuts to seek safe voyages. The sibling bond ritual remains central, but the coastal variations emphasize the monsoon and the sea.
