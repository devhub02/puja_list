## Summary
Festival celebrating Lord Krishna's protection of the cowherds and cattle by lifting Govardhan Mountain. Celebrated with offerings of food, cow dung, and stones.

## Significance
Govardhan Puja celebrates Lord Krishna's divine intervention to protect his community (gopis and cows) from heavy rains and floods. According to the Bhagavata Purana, Krishna lifted the Govardhan Mountain as an umbrella. This festival also marks the beginning of Annakut (mount of food), where food offerings symbolize abundance and gratitude for the harvest.

## Vidhi (Steps)
**Step 1: Create a Govardhan Mound**
Prepare a mound or representation of Govardhan Mountain using dried cow dung. This can be done in a courtyard, temple, or designated space. The mound symbolizes the sacred mountain that Krishna lifted. Decorate the mound with flowers and place small pebbles on it.

**Step 2: Light Lamps Around the Mound**
Arrange oil lamps (diyas) in a circle around the Govardhan mound. Light each diya with devotion. The lamps symbolize the protection and divine light that Krishna provided. As you light each lamp, recite prayers invoking Krishna's blessings.

**Step 3: Offer Flowers and Water**
Offer flowers and water to the Govardhan mound. These offerings represent respect and gratitude to the mountain and to Krishna for his protection. Recite prayers or chants as you make these offerings. If available, sprinkle water around the mound in a clockwise direction.

**Step 4: Circumambulate the Mound**
Walk around the Govardhan mound in a clockwise direction. This ritual, called pradakshina, is a form of worship and respect. Many people perform this while singing devotional songs or chanting Krishna's names. The circumambulation symbolizes one's devotion and gratitude to the divine.

**Step 5: Prepare and Offer Annakut (Mount of Food)**
Prepare a large variety of food offerings, called Annakut. This can include sweets, savory dishes, fruits, and vegetables. Arrange them in an attractive display to symbolize abundance and gratitude for the harvest. Offer these foods to the deity with prayers. After the puja, distribute the blessed food (prasad) among family and friends.

**Step 6: Celebrate with Cow/Cattle Worship (Optional)**
If a cow or cattle are available, perform a simple worship ritual with them. Apply tilak or vermillion on their foreheads, offer flowers, and feed them with the blessed food. Cows are revered in Hindu tradition as representing Krishna himself. This practice honors the animals that are central to Krishna's pastoral life and the meaning of Govardhan.

## Regional Variations
**Mathura and Brindavan Tradition** (Regions: north)
In Mathura and Brindavan (places closely associated with Lord Krishna), Govardhan Puja is celebrated with extraordinary grandeur. Govardhan Mountain itself becomes the center of worship, with thousands of devotees circumambulating it. The festival is marked by elaborate Annakut celebrations with vast quantities of food offerings. Traditional music, dance, and Krishna devotional songs (bhajans) are performed throughout the day.
