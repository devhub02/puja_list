## Summary
Celebration of the birth of Lord Krishna, the eighth avatar of Vishnu. The puja is marked by devotional singing, playing with butter, and midnight celebrations when Krishna is believed to be born.

## Significance
Janmashtami celebrates the birth of Lord Krishna, the eighth avatar of Lord Vishnu. Krishna is revered for his divine wisdom, unconditional love, compassion, and his role in the Mahabharata and Bhagavad Gita. The puja is an expression of joy and devotion. Many temples organize grand celebrations with cultural performances, devotional songs, and enactments of Krishna's life.

## Vidhi (Steps)
**Step 1: Purification and Setup**
Clean and decorate the puja space with flowers and festive decorations. Arrange the Krishna idol on the altar, preferably in a cradle or crib (to symbolize his birth). Light diyas around the space to create a festive and sacred atmosphere.

**Step 2: Apply Tilak**
Apply turmeric and vermillion tilak on your forehead and on the idol, marking the sacred celebration.

**Step 3: Burn Incense**
Light incense and move it in circles before the idol, creating a sacred and fragrant space for the celebration.

**Step 4: Offer Flowers and Prayers**
Offer flowers and prayers to Lord Krishna. Speak your intentions and seek his blessings for love, joy, wisdom, and divine grace. Recite prayers or mantras as per your family tradition.

**Step 5: Meditation and Devotional Singing**
Sit in meditation before the idol. Visualize Lord Krishna in your heart. Sing devotional songs (bhajans) about Krishna's life, his divine plays (leelas), and his teachings. Devotional music and singing are central to Janmashtami celebrations.

**Step 6: Midnight Celebration (if possible) (Optional)**
If timing allows, perform the main puja at midnight, as Krishna is believed to be born at midnight. At this time, offer special prayers, light more diyas, and perform the aarti. If midnight is not possible, perform the puja during the day with equal devotion.

**Step 7: Offer Butter and Sweets**
Offer butter (makhan) and sweets to Krishna. Butter is Krishna's favorite food, and offering it playfully (as children do) is a cherished part of Janmashtami. Place butter and sweets at the feet of the idol, and some devotees playfully feed butter to the idol.

**Step 8: Aarti**
Perform aarti by waving the lamp in front of the idol in a circular motion. The aarti expresses gratitude for Krishna's birth and the joy he brings to the world.

**Step 9: Seek Blessings**
Respectfully bow to the idol and seek Krishna's blessings for love, joy, spiritual growth, and protection. Touch your forehead to receive his blessings.

**Step 10: Distribute Prasad**
Distribute the butter and sweets as prasad to all family members and guests. Prasad carries Krishna's blessings and is believed to bring joy and divine grace. The distribution is done in celebration of Krishna's birth.

## Regional Variations
**Mathura and Brindavan Tradition** (Regions: north)
In Mathura and Brindavan, Krishna's birthplace and childhood home, Janmashtami is celebrated with grand processions, elaborate decorations, and community feasts. The temples organize special midnight prayers and enactments of Krishna's birth story.

**Raas Leela Celebrations** (Regions: pan_india)
In some regions, particularly in North India, Janmashtami includes Raas Leela (the cosmic dance) performances where devotees enact Krishna's divine love story with Gopis (milkmaids). These theatrical presentations are forms of devotional expression.
