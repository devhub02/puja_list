## Summary
Worship ritual for Lord Ganesh on his birthday, seeking blessings for the removal of obstacles and the success of new endeavors.

## Significance
Ganesh Chaturthi celebrates the birth of Lord Ganesh, the most widely worshipped deity in Hinduism. Ganesh is invoked at the beginning of all auspicious rituals as the remover of obstacles (Vighnaharta). This puja seeks his blessings for wisdom, success, and the removal of all impediments from life.

## Vidhi (Steps)
**Step 1: Purification and Setup**
Clean and prepare the puja space. Arrange the Ganesh idol and samagri items on the altar. Sit in a comfortable position facing the idol.

**Step 2: Light the Diya**
Light the diya using oil and set it in front of the altar. The lamp symbolizes divine knowledge and dispels darkness.

**Step 3: Offer Flowers and Rice**
Offer fresh flowers and rice grains at the feet of the Ganesh idol, expressing devotion and respect.

**Step 4: Apply Tilak**
Apply turmeric and vermillion tilak on the idol's forehead and on yourself. This marks the sacred nature of the ritual.

**Step 5: Burn Incense**
Light agarbatti (incense) and move it in circles in front of the idol. The fragrance purifies the space and aids concentration.

**Step 6: Meditation and Prayers**
Sit quietly and meditate on Lord Ganesh. Offer prayers seeking his blessings for wisdom, success, and the removal of obstacles. Recite mantras or prayers as per your family tradition.

**Step 7: Offer Modak**
Offer modak and other sweets at the feet of the idol. Modak is Ganesh's favorite offering and symbolizes sweetness and joy.

**Step 8: Aarti**
Perform aarti by waving the lamp or camphor flame in front of the idol in a circular motion. This signifies the completion of the puja and gratitude to the deity.

**Step 9: Seek Blessings**
Respectfully bow to the idol and seek Ganesh's blessings. Touch the altar and then your forehead as a gesture of receiving blessings.

**Step 10: Distribute Prasad**
Distribute the offered food (modak, sweets, coconut) as prasad among family members and guests. Prasad carries the blessings of the deity.

## Regional Variations
**Maharashtra Tradition** (Regions: west)
In Maharashtra, the puja is followed by a 10-day celebration. On the final day (Anant Chaturdashi), the idol is immersed in water (Ganesh Visarjan). Modak is the main offering throughout the festival.

**South Indian Tradition** (Regions: south)
In South India, the puja includes the preparation of neyyappam (sweet preparations) and the application of kumkum (vermillion) is more prominent. The festival duration may vary by region and local customs.
