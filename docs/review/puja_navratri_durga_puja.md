## Summary
Nine-day festival celebrating Goddess Durga and her victory over evil. Kalash Sthapana (pot installation) marks the beginning of Navratri, symbolizing the manifestation of divine energy and the planting of seeds for prosperity.

## Significance
Navratri celebrates the nine forms (Navadurga) of Goddess Durga. Each day represents a different aspect of the goddess and her divine powers. Kalash Sthapana on the first day marks the beginning of the festival and represents the awakening of divine feminine energy. The germinating barley (jau) inside the kalash symbolizes prosperity and abundance. Durga is worshipped for her power to destroy evil and protect her devotees.

## Vidhi (Steps)
**Step 1: Purification and Preparation**
Clean and purify the puja space. Select a clean location, preferably facing east or north. Gather all materials for the Kalash Sthapana. Sit in a comfortable meditative posture.

**Step 2: Prepare the Kalash**
Fill the kalash with holy soil (preferably from a temple or sacred place). Fill it three-quarters full. Add barley seeds on top of the soil. Pour water carefully so that the seeds are moist but not waterlogged. The kalash should be placed on a raised platform or stand.

**Step 3: Light the Diyas**
Light multiple diyas around and near the kalash. The light represents the divine presence of Goddess Durga and the victory of light over darkness.

**Step 4: Apply Tilak**
Apply turmeric and vermillion tilak on your forehead, marking the beginning of the sacred Navratri festival.

**Step 5: Burn Incense**
Light incense and move it in circles in front of the kalash. The fragrance purifies the space and invokes the divine presence.

**Step 6: Invocation of the Goddess**
Offer flowers, rice, and prayers to invoke Goddess Durga. Speak your intentions for the nine-day festival. Seek her blessings for victory over obstacles, strength, and prosperity.

**Step 7: Meditation and Prayers**
Sit in quiet meditation before the kalash. Visualize the divine energy of Goddess Durga manifesting through the kalash. Meditate on her nine forms (Nav Durga) and seek her blessings for the festival period. Recite prayers or mantras as per your tradition.

**Step 8: Aarti**
Perform aarti by waving the lamp in front of the kalash in a circular motion. The aarti represents gratitude and the welcoming of divine energy into your home and life.

**Step 9: Daily Care of Kalash**
During the nine days of Navratri, keep the kalash in a clean, safe place. Water the seeds daily so they sprout into golden barley shoots (jau). Light diyas before the kalash each day. On the ninth day (Dussehra or Vijayadashami), the sprouted barley shoots are distributed as prasad.

**Step 10: Seek Blessings**
Respectfully bow to the kalash and seek Goddess Durga's blessings for strength, victory, and protection. Touch your forehead to the ground as a gesture of complete surrender.

## Regional Variations
**Eastern India - Bengal Tradition** (Regions: east)
In Bengal, Navratri is celebrated as Durga Puja with grand community celebrations, elaborate pandals, and artistic displays. The worship focuses on Goddess Durga's nine forms, and the festival includes cultural programs, processions, and community feasting.

**South Indian Tradition** (Regions: south)
In South India, Navratri is celebrated with Golu (display of idols) in homes, Dandiya and Garba dances (in some regions), and the reading of Devi Mahatmya. The worship includes rituals specific to the region and local goddesses.
