## Summary
Night-long worship of Lord Shiva celebrated on the 14th night of the dark fortnight of Phalgun. Seeks blessings for spiritual growth and protection.

## Significance
Maha Shivratri celebrates the power and glory of Lord Shiva, the supreme being in Hindu philosophy. It is believed that on this night, Shiva performed the cosmic dance (Tandava). The puja involves all-night vigil, meditation, and fasting. Devotees seek Shiva's blessings for spiritual awakening, destruction of ego, and ultimate liberation.

## Vidhi (Steps)
**Step 1: Purification and Setup**
Clean and prepare the puja space. Set up the Shiva lingam on an altar with a base (usually a stone or metal container). Arrange all samagri items around the lingam. Light the diya and incense. Sit in a comfortable meditation posture facing the lingam.

**Step 2: Ritual Bathing of the Lingam**
Perform the ritual bathing (abhisheka) of the Shiva lingam. Begin by bathing it with water, followed by milk, yogurt, ghee, and honey in sequence. Each bathing is a form of worship and purification. Rinse with water at the end.

**Step 3: Apply Tilak**
Apply turmeric and vermillion tilak on yourself as a mark of participation in the sacred ritual. This signifies your dedication to Lord Shiva.

**Step 4: Offer Bilva Leaves**
Offer fresh bilva leaves on the lingam. Bilva leaves are sacred to Shiva and are considered the most important offering. Offer them with devotion and prayers. The leaves can be offered in groups of three (representing the three aspects of Shiva) or as individual leaves.

**Step 5: Offer Flowers (Optional)**
Offer flowers at the base of the lingam as a symbol of devotion and beauty. Flowers represent the beauty of creation and the divine.

**Step 6: All-Night Vigil and Meditation**
Engage in an all-night vigil dedicated to Lord Shiva. Spend the night in meditation, chanting, or silent prayer. Keep the diyas lit throughout the night. Some devotees recite scriptures, particularly the Shiva Purana or Shiva Chalisa. Maintain focus on spiritual practices and avoid sleep to honor Shiva.

**Step 7: Renewal of Offerings (Optional)**
During the night, periodically offer fresh bilva leaves and other offerings to the lingam. This can be done at midnight or during the early morning hours. Each offering is a gesture of continuous devotion.

**Step 8: Aarti at Dawn**
As dawn approaches, perform the aarti by waving the lamp in front of the lingam in a circular motion. The aarti marks the completion of the all-night vigil and gratitude to Lord Shiva. This is the main aarti of the puja.

**Step 9: Seek Blessings**
Respectfully bow to the lingam and seek Shiva's blessings for spiritual growth, protection, and liberation. Touch your forehead to the ground as a gesture of complete surrender and devotion.

**Step 10: Prasad Distribution (Optional)**
Distribute prasad (blessed offerings) to family members and others. Traditionally, fruit, milk-based sweets, or simple offerings are distributed. Prasad carries Shiva's blessings to all who partake.

## Regional Variations
**Fasting Tradition** (Regions: pan_india)
Many devotees observe a fast (vrat) on Maha Shivratri. There are different types of fasts: complete abstinence from food, consuming only fruits and milk, or consuming specific foods. People with health conditions, pregnant women, or those on medication should consult a doctor before fasting. Breaking the fast should be done after the puja is complete.

**North Indian Tradition** (Regions: north)
In North India, particularly in Uttar Pradesh and the Himalayas, Maha Shivratri is celebrated with grand processions and community gatherings at Shiva temples. The worship of the lingam remains central, and large bonfires (havan) are often lit during the night.
